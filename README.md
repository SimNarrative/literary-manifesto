## NOT “LET THERE BE”, BUT FORGED

Sim-narrative was not born in the quiet of an office under the rustle of pages. It did not descend as a revelation from above. It crawled out layer by layer from chaos, from reflection and analysis, from dozens of rewritten paragraphs.

I wrote for the drawer. But the drawer was in my head.

There, in the darkness, lived characters, events, fragments of dialogue. They begged to be let out, but could not take shape. I poured out drafts — raw, blind, screaming. And then the main thing happened: I began to analyze them. Not as a rewriter, but as a researcher.

And it began.

The first story born in sim-narrative drained me dry. Forty versions. Forty times I returned to the beginning, reshaped motivations, analyzed why the hero acted this way and not otherwise. A year ago the method was so-so — it didn’t work, it fell apart, it talked nonsense. But in these reflections, in this battle for every phrase, something new began to sprout.

The layer of reflection was not born out of thin air. It was born from the question: “What does he *really* feel?”

I analyzed the motives of actions, and suddenly it turned out: the character has something to say to himself. Not in dialogue, not in action — in silence, in a monologue addressed to himself. That is how the second layer appeared.

The third layer — log, report, system record — arose from the analysis of facts.

I reflected and analyzed what reality stood behind the events. Where does the action take place? What are its laws? What do documents, statistics, history say? And one day I understood: let the system speak for itself. Coldly, detachedly, without emotion. That is how the third layer was born.

There are no accidental insights. There is no magical “LET THERE BE SIM-NARRATIVE FROM NOW ON!”

It is **labor**. Long, persistent, often painful. It is when you redo one page forty times and still are not sure. It is when your own thoughts disobey, slip away, go into the shadows and return only after the hundredth question. It is when characters disobey, resist, go into the shadows and return only after the hundredth question.

Sim-narrative is documented creative work. Its value lies not so much in a beautiful theory as in the fact that each layer appeared from long reflection, trial, and analysis.

Those who continue this work should remember: none of this arises by itself. The path requires time, patience, and a readiness to grapple with difficulties.

## DEFINITION

Sim-narrative is not just a genre. It is a state of writing, a literary-narrative ecosystem born in the process of multi-layered authorial research. Here the very architecture of the narrative becomes the plot, the method, and the final work.

Sim-narrative is not “a text written according to a template.” It is stories written by a human being, thoughts passed through human consciousness and soul, and refined in repeated reworking. It is the documentation of a living process where different voices meet in order to give birth to a third, impossible separately.

The method was created by the author on the basis of many years of reflection, trial, error, and analysis.

## THE THREE PILLARS OF SIM-NARRATIVE

### 1. THE SELF-REFLEXIVE LOOP

The work is aware of and explores its own nature. How was it created? Who is the author? What is the “I” in the system of narration? Meta-questions are woven into the fabric of the text, not relegated to a preface.

### 2. MULTI-LAYERED ARCHITECTURE

A rejection of a single linear narrative in favor of a symphony of registers. The base structure is the **“Triplex”** (three layers), but there can be more.

| Layer | Name | Essence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| First | Artistic | Scene, image, event, emotion. What is usually called “literature.” |
| Second | Reflexive | Inner monologue, personal experience, character’s diary, confession. Born from reflection and analysis of the motives of actions. |
| Third | Systemic | A cold external view: report, log, statistics, briefing. Born from the analysis of facts and reality. |
| Fourth | Meta-layer (the author’s path) | How did the author come to this plot? What patterns, events, thoughts led to its birth? It may be one line, or it may be a separate work. The fourth layer is you yourself, stepping out of the shadows. |
| Fifth (experimental) | Procedural | Documentation of the living process: drafts, questions, reflections and analysis, insights, the search for form. The very process of the birth of sim-narrative becomes part of it. The fifth layer is the path of the text to itself. |

There may be other numbers of layers: two, four, five. They may be called something else. The principle is important: **polyphony of subjectivities**.

### 3. REFLECTION AND ANALYSIS AS ONTOLOGY

Reflection and analysis are not just tools. They are a way of existence for the text. Inner analysis between layers, between author and character, between event and its description. The boundary between voices is fluid. Each layer is a participant, a witness, an object of analysis.

Sim-narrative is a tree with many branches. Here are the first of them:

| Branch | Subject of research | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Technosim | The fusion of the physical and the digital. Neurointerfaces, cyborgization, mind uploading. | “Digital Hell” |

## PRACTICE (HOW TO WORK WITH THE METHOD)

1. **Start with an event.** Not with an idea, not with a concept — with a concrete scene that begs to be let out.
2. **Write the artistic layer.** Let the event happen. Don’t explain — show.
3. **Ask: what does the character really feel?** Write the reflexive layer. Let him speak to himself, without witnesses.
4. **Ask: how does this event look from the outside?** Write the systemic layer — report, log, statistics, briefing. Coldly, detachedly, without emotion.
5. **Look at the path by which you came to this plot.** This is the meta-layer. It may be one line, or it may be a separate work.
6. **If you wish, document the process.** Drafts, questions, reflections and analysis, insights, the search for form. This is the procedural layer. The very process of the birth of sim-narrative becomes part of it.
7. **Rewrite. Do not accept the first version.** Seek resonance between the layers. One phrase may require forty versions.

## WHY THIS MATTERS

We stand on the threshold of a new anthropology — when the “I” expands beyond the boundaries of the biological body. Sim-narrative is a literary laboratory for this expansion. It is not an escape from the human, but a radical deepening of it through the comprehension of the Other.

We are creating not just stories. We are creating a language for talking about a future that has already arrived. And every new layer we open — whether the fourth or the fifth — is not complication for complication’s sake. It is an attempt to hold in the text the full depth of human (and non-human) experience.

Sim-narrative teaches us to see farther. Different voices learn from one another. Together we begin to discern patterns where before we saw only chaos. And this is not magic. It is a new way of thinking.

## Afterword. On the Place of Sim-Narrative in the Literary Tradition

Every new form in literature does not arise from emptiness. It takes shape at the intersection of already existing practices — sometimes long known, but seen in a new light.

Multi-layered narrative is not new. Twentieth-century literature widely explored polyphony, separated voices, inner monologues, unreliable narrators, documentary and pseudo-documentary forms. In various traditions — from the modernist novel to post-documentary prose — there were attempts to show a person simultaneously from within and from without.

Nevertheless, these techniques rarely became the subject of separate discussion as a structural principle that can be consciously singled out, described, and offered for consideration.

More often they remained part of an individual author’s technique.

Sim-narrative arises precisely at this point — as an attempt to do what previously existed in scattered form: to single out the multi-layeredness of narrative as an independent object of attention.

This is not about creating a new genre or revising the literary tradition. Rather, it is a proposal to consider certain texts as a special form of composition, where several perspectives coexist simultaneously and are meaningfully separated.

If in a traditional story event, experience, and external view are often merged into a single stream, here they retain their separateness. The reader encounters not one interpretation of events, but several registers describing the same reality.

This way of writing requires a different way of reading. Meaning is not transmitted directly — it is assembled from juxtaposition.

In this respect, sim-narrative is closer to montage than to linear narrative: elements do not explain each other, but enter into relation.

Why this conversation arises precisely now is a question that inevitably leads beyond literature.

Modern man increasingly exists simultaneously in several forms of description:

* as a participant in an event;
* as its interpreter;
* as an object of external observation — statistical, institutional, digital.

What formerly could remain in the background is gradually entering everyday experience. Every action is recorded, analyzed, turned into data. Every story can be told simultaneously as personal and as systemic.

Literature, as has happened more than once in its history, begins to respond to this change.

Sim-narrative can be considered as one of the attempts at artistic comprehension of this new state — when human experience turns out to be distributed among different ways of describing it.

It is important to emphasize: this is not about opposing these ways. None is declared more “true.” They simply exist side by side.

If this text contains any proposal, it is a modest one: to try to look at multi-layeredness not as a private technique, but as a principle of organizing narrative, worthy of separate discussion.

Perhaps in time such texts will prove to be merely one episode in the long history of literary experiments. Perhaps, on the contrary, this approach will prove productive and find development in other forms.

In any case, sim-narrative does not claim to be a final solution. Rather, it captures a moment when accumulated intuitions — literary, cultural, technological — begin to form into a recognizable shape.

If so, this text has a simple function: it marks a point of conversation.

Everything else is a matter of time and of those who wish to continue the experiment.
